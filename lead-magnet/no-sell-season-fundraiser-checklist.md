# The No-Sell Season Fundraiser Checklist

**Fund your whole season without selling, storing or delivering a single thing.**

For coaches, team parents and booster clubs who are done with cookie dough, car washes and cash envelopes.

**How to use it:** work through the 8 steps in order. Most teams finish setup in one evening. Tick each box as you go, then share this page in your team group chat so every family knows the plan.

## Step 1: Set a season goal, then break it down per player

A big team number feels impossible. A per-player number feels doable, and families can see exactly what's asked of them.

- [ ] Add up the season: tournaments, travel, uniforms, gear, field time, officials
- [ ] Subtract what dues already cover
- [ ] Divide what's left by the number of players
- [ ] Divide the per-player goal by a typical gift to see how many gifts each player needs

**Example:**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Season costs not covered by dues | $10,000 |
| Players on the roster | 20 |
| Goal per player | $500 |
| Typical gift from family | about $40 |
| Gifts each player needs | about 12 or 13 |

Twelve gifts is grandparents, aunts, uncles, a godparent and a few family friends. That's one evening of texting, not a month of selling.

## Step 2: Ask 3 questions before any app touches your team's money

Some online fundraising companies keep up to 20% of what your families raise, and some donors find surprise add-ons on their card statements. Ask every option these three questions, and get the answers in writing.

- [ ] **Where does the money land?** In your team's own bank account, or in the company's account first?
- [ ] **What's the fee, total?** Including card processing, tips and any "platform" charge. Ask: "If a donor gives $100, how much reaches our team?"
- [ ] **Is anything pre-checked at checkout?** Tips, add-ons and merchandise should be the donor's choice, never a box that's already ticked.

**Bonus question:** Will you email our families after the season ends, or ask our kids for contact lists? The right answer is no.

## Step 3: Give every player their own page

Grandma doesn't want to give to "the team." She wants to give to *her* grandkid. A page for each player turns a general ask into a personal one.

- [ ] Each page has the player's photo (parents' choice), first name and a one-line story: "I made 12U this year and we're going to nationals"
- [ ] Each page shows that player's goal and a progress bar
- [ ] Each page has its own share link and QR code
- [ ] Parents claim their child's page and approve what's shown
- [ ] Parent and donor emails stay off public pages

Keep it simple: last name optional, no school or home details, no pressure leaderboards.

## Step 4: Send the 3-text ask to family

Most families don't mind giving. What they hate is figuring out what to say. Hand them these three texts to copy, paste and send, ideally from the player's own phone or with their voice in it.

- [ ] Each family picks 10–15 people: grandparents, aunts, uncles, godparents, close family friends
- [ ] Send text 1 on launch day
- [ ] Send text 2 about a week later, only to people who haven't given yet
- [ ] Send text 3 to everyone who gave, when the goal is hit

**Text 1: The ask (launch day)**

> Hi Grandma! I made the \[team name\] this season and we're playing in \[tournament / city\]. Here's my page with my goal: \[link\]. It would mean a lot if you cheered me on. ❤️

**Text 2: The nudge (one week later)**

> We're \[halfway / almost there\] on our team goal! Thanks to everyone cheering us on. Here's my page if you'd like to help: \[link\]

**Text 3: The thank-you (goal hit)**

> We did it! Thank you so much for helping me play this season. Here's a picture from \[game / tournament\]. \[photo\]

Three texts. No catalog. No spam. Just family cheering on a kid.

## Step 5: Line up 3 local sponsors

Local businesses want to be seen supporting kids, and their customers are your families. Three sponsors can cover a real chunk of the season.

- [ ] List 10 businesses your families already use: the pizza place after games, a dentist, a realtor, an auto shop, a gym
- [ ] Start with businesses owned by team parents or alumni
- [ ] Offer three simple tiers (set the dollar amounts for your area)
- [ ] Ask in person with a one-page sheet and a specific number
- [ ] Send each sponsor a team photo with their name or logo by mid-season

**Example tiers**

| Tier | Example amount | What the sponsor gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | $250 | Name and link on the team page, one social media thank-you |
| Silver | $500 | Logo on the team page and every player page, a shout-out at a home game |
| Gold | $1,000 | Everything in Silver, plus logo on the banner or QR poster at the field |

**The 20-second ask**

> Hi, I'm \[name\], a parent on the \[team\]. Our players are raising money for \[tournaments / travel / uniforms\] this season. We'd love to have \[business\] as a sponsor. For \[amount\], your logo goes on our team page and every player's page, and families see it every time they share. Can I leave this sheet with you?

## Step 6: Set up a private fund so no kid sits out

Youth sports keep getting more expensive: families now spend about $1,016 a year on a child's main sport, up 46% since 2019 (Aspen Institute Project Play). A team fund lets the community cover the gap without anyone being singled out.

- [ ] Create a separate "No kid sits out" fund on your team page
- [ ] Decide what it covers: dues, travel, gear, tournament fees
- [ ] Decide who approves requests: the coach plus one board member or treasurer
- [ ] Never name or hint at which families get help
- [ ] Tell families quietly and privately that help exists, so nobody has to ask in front of others
- [ ] Mention the fund to sponsors and grandparents; it's often the easiest ask of the season

**Check with your treasurer** on how the fund is recorded, and whether gifts are tax-deductible (only if your club is a qualifying nonprofit).

## Step 7: Print a QR poster for the field or studio

Some of your best donors are standing right there: parents, grandparents, alumni and neighbors in the bleachers. A poster lets them give in seconds from their phone.

- [ ] Print one large poster with the team QR code, the season goal and one line: "Scan to support our players"
- [ ] Put it where people wait: the fence, the concession stand, the studio lobby, the gym door
- [ ] Ask sponsor businesses to tape a small one by their register
- [ ] Add the QR code to game programs, recital flyers and the team banner
- [ ] Test the code on two different phones before printing

## Step 8: Thank donors and show what their money paid for

A donor who sees what their gift did gives again next season. A donor who hears nothing usually doesn't.

- [ ] Make sure every donor gets an instant receipt by email
- [ ] Have each player send text 3 (from Step 4) when the goal is hit
- [ ] Post one team photo showing what the money paid for: the new uniforms, the tournament, the bus
- [ ] Thank sponsors publicly on social media and at a home game
- [ ] At season's end, share a short recap with the families: total raised, what it covered, how many kids the fund helped
- [ ] Write down what worked so next season's setup takes half the time

## The checklist at a glance

- [ ] **1.** Set a season goal and a per-player number
- [ ] **2.** Ask the 3 money questions before you pick a tool
- [ ] **3.** Give every player their own page
- [ ] **4.** Send the 3-text ask to family
- [ ] **5.** Line up 3 local sponsors
- [ ] **6.** Set up a private "No kid sits out" fund
- [ ] **7.** Print a QR poster for the field
- [ ] **8.** Thank donors and show what their money paid for

No selling. No cash envelopes. No lost Saturdays.
