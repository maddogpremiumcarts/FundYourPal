# FundYourPal Partner Email Series

Oct 28, 2026

Five short emails for partners to send to their lists, each driving coaches and team parents to download the checklist on the FundYourPal opt-in page.

## Before you send

The page at [fundyourpal.com/for-teams](https://www.fundyourpal.com/for-teams) has no checklist and no email form today; every button goes straight to sign-up. These emails promise a free checklist, so add a form and the checklist before any partner mails them. Otherwise you get clicks but no emails.

### The offer these emails sell

**The No-Sell Season Fundraiser Checklist**: a free one-page checklist for coaches and team parents to fund the whole season without selling anything. It's a working title, so change the name if you already have one. Suggested contents, all drawn from our research:

1. Set a season goal and break it down per player
2. Ask 3 questions before any app touches your team's money (where it lands, the total fee, any add-ons at checkout)
3. Give every player their own page with a photo and goal
4. Send the 3-text ask that gets grandparents to give
5. Line up 3 local sponsors with simple tiers
6. Set up a private fund so no kid sits out over money
7. Print a QR poster for the field or studio
8. Thank donors and show them what their money paid for

### What the opt-in page needs

- [ ] A form near the top asking for **first name and email only**, plus an optional "Team or club" field
- [ ] A button that names the reward: "Send me the checklist"
- [ ] The checklist delivered instantly, by email and on a thank-you page
- [ ] On the thank-you page, a soft next step: "Ready now? Start your team in 5 minutes"
- [ ] A plain privacy line: "We'll email you the checklist and a few tips. Unsubscribe anytime."

### How partner mailings work

These emails go out from the partner's own account (a league, a booster club association, a coaching newsletter, a sports-parent creator), so their readers already trust the sender. Each email is written so the partner can send it as is, signed with their name, or as a short "from our friends at FundYourPal" note. The partner's own email platform handles the unsubscribe link and mailing address. Readers join FundYourPal's list only when they fill out the form.

## The voice: "the parent who's run every fundraiser"

These emails sound like the veteran team parent at the end of the bleachers: has done the cookie dough, counted the cash envelopes, and now just tells you what works. Short, warm, a little dry, never salesy. The authority comes from knowing the reader's week better than they expect, not from claims.

**Rules every email follows**

- **One email, one idea, one link.** Every click goes to the checklist.
- **Sell the checklist, not the software.** FundYourPal is named lightly, mostly in the sign-off. The reader is opting in for help, not a demo.
- **Under 200 words.** Most coaches read on their phone between practice and dinner.
- **Plain text look.** No banners or graphics; it should read like a note from a person.
- **Their words:** players, team mom, the group chat, cookie dough, the same three parents, travel ball, comp season. Never "participants," "leverage" or "maximize."
- **No fake stories.** The emails use "most teams" and real public examples, never invented customers.
- **Name the sender.** Replace \[Sender name\] with a real person; emails from a person beat emails from a brand.

## Email 3: Where the money goes

**Job:** build trust by teaching the reader how to judge any fundraising company, then let the checklist do the selling.

**Subject line options:** "3 questions to ask before any app touches your team's money" / "How much of your fundraiser actually reaches the team?"

**Preview text:** Most coaches never ask the third one.

> Hi \[First name\],
>
> Before any fundraising company touches your team's money, ask three questions:
>
> **1. Where does the money land?** In your team's own bank account, or in theirs first?
>
> **2. What's the fee, total?** Platform fee, card processing, any "tip": add it all up. The real number.
>
> **3. Is anything pre-checked at checkout?** Grandma shouldn't pay for a hoodie she didn't ask for.
>
> Some online fundraising companies keep up to a fifth of everything your families raise. And some donors have found surprise add-ons on their card statements.
>
> Your team works too hard for that.
>
> These three questions are step 2 of **The No-Sell Season Fundraiser Checklist**, along with seven more steps to fund your season honestly.
>
> [**Get the free checklist →**](https://www.fundyourpal.com/for-teams)
>
> &#91;Sender name\]
>
> P.S. With FundYourPal, the answers are: your team's own bank, 10% total (we even pay the card processing fees), and nothing pre-checked. Donors can choose to cover the 10% so the team gets every dollar.

*Fact check:* "up to a fifth" refers to published 20% platform fees; the add-on charges come from public donor reviews. Confirm the P.S. matches your product and Terms before sending.

## Sending notes

### Cadence

| Email | Send on | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Cookie-dough confession | Day 1 | Broadest hook; warms the list |
| 2. Same three parents | Day 3 | Hits the volunteer who'll act |
| 3. Where the money goes | Day 5 | Trust and authority |
| 4. No kid sits out | Day 8 | Emotional peak |
| 5. Last call | Day 10 | Direct close |

A good first test: Tuesday to Thursday evenings, after practice and dinner. Adjust once you see each partner's results. Time the series for 2–4 weeks before a season starts (late summer for fall sports, January for spring).

### Partner intro line

If the partner wants to introduce us instead of sending as their own note, add this above email 1:

> I don't usually pass along stuff like this, but so many of you have asked me about fundraising that I wanted to share it. Our friends at FundYourPal put together a free checklist that's worth five minutes of your time.

### Tracking

Give each partner and each email its own tracking link, for example:

`https://www.fundyourpal.com/for-teams?utm_source=[partner]&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=checklist&utm_content=email1`

### What to measure

- **Opt-ins per partner**, not opens: the only number that grows your list
- **Click-to-opt-in rate** on the page: if lots of people click but few sign up, the page or form needs work, not the emails
- **Which email drives the most opt-ins**: lead with that subject line for the next partner
- **Sign-ups from checklist subscribers** over the following 30 days
