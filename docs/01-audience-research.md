# FundYourPal Audience Research

Oct 22, 2026

## The big picture

Your market is exhausted, squeezed and suspicious, and that is your opening. Families now spend about $1,016 a year on one child's main sport, up 46% since 2019, so every club leans harder on fundraising. But parents hate selling cookie dough, donors resent overpriced products, and the dominant online platform keeps up to 20% and is now facing a class action over hidden donor fees.

The core desire across all three audiences is the same: **"Let me help the kids without the hassle, the guilt, or the feeling that someone in the middle is skimming."** FundYourPal answers that with three things competitors struggle to say together: set up yourself in 5 minutes, a flat 10% with no monthly fee, and money that lands in the club's own Stripe account.

The one thing to remember when writing anything: you are not selling software. You are selling the end of the cookie-dough era, and the relief of a treasurer who never has to explain where the money went.

## Who you're really selling to

Three people touch every fundraiser, but only one signs up. Market to the organizer; design your sharing tools and pages for the parent and the donor.

| Audience | Role in the sale | Who they are | What they want in one line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Organizer** | Buyer. Signs up, connects Stripe, imports the roster | Coach, team mom/dad, booster or PTO president, club treasurer, dance/cheer/band director | "Hit our goal without becoming a full-time fundraiser or getting blamed for the money" |
| **The Parent** | User. Claims the kid's page and shares it | Busy working parent, often both parents working | "Let my kid play without me selling junk to my coworkers" |
| **The Donor** | Payer. Gives $25–$50 | Grandparents, aunts and uncles, family friends, coworkers, local businesses | "Help this specific kid, quickly, and know the money actually gets there" |

Your own live page proves the donor profile: recent gifts come from "Auntie Gail," "MeMa n PopPop" and "G Pa," mostly at $25. That is a family-and-grandparents audience, and your donor-facing copy should sound like it.

**Primary segments to target first:**

- Volunteer-run community clubs and rec leagues (no paid staff, no admin help)
- High school booster clubs (sports, band, cheer, theatre)
- Travel and tournament teams (the fastest-growing cost pressure)
- Arts programs: dance studios, choirs, robotics and academic clubs (your site already says "every performer and student")

## The Organizer: coaches, booster leaders, treasurers

The organizer is a tired volunteer carrying the whole program on a few shoulders, and their deepest fear is being blamed. They did not sign up to coach so they could run a sales operation.

### Pains (what keeps them up at night)

- **Doing it all alone.** Booster and PTA circles joke that the same few people do everything; one booster secretary said six or eight volunteers do the work for 1,400 families ([HeraldNet](https://www.heraldnet.com/?p=264737)). Burnout is the norm.
- **Fundraising takes over coaching.** Product sales mean order forms, cash envelopes, inventory, deliveries and chasing late families.
- **Parents push back.** Parents complain about being asked to sell, and many quietly skip it. The organizer absorbs that resentment.
- **Money-handling risk and suspicion.** Cash and checks create opportunities for mistakes and for theft. In September 2026 a California booster treasurer was charged with taking $411,761 from a football club and hiding it with false reports ([Fox LA](https://www.foxla.com/news/aliso-niguel-high-school-booster-club-treasurer-wire-fraud-arrest.amp)). Every honest treasurer now feels watched.
- **Fees eating the goal.** One Portland booster leader raised $16,000 through a platform taking $1 of every $5, and the school district's IT team pushed back on that cut ([Willamette Week](https://wweek.com/news/2022/04/06/portland-high-school-football-boosters-are-using-a-seattle-software-the-school-district-banned)).
- **Sales reps and slow setup.** Many platforms route you through a rep, a demo call or a fixed 28-day campaign.
- **Kids who can't afford to play.** Coaches see the player who quietly stops showing up because of dues, gear or travel.

### Fears (what they won't say out loud)

- "What if we don't hit the goal and the season gets cut?"
- "What if a parent accuses me of mishandling money?"
- "What if I pick the wrong platform and look foolish in front of the board?"
- "What if this new tool is a scam, or sells our families' emails?"

### Wants (practical, rational)

- Setup in minutes, no approval call, no contract
- Clear, flat fees they can explain to the board in one sentence
- Money in their own account, with a record for every dollar
- One link and QR code to hand to parents
- Goals and progress they can show at a parent meeting
- Sponsor spots that local businesses will actually pay for

### Desires (emotional, aspirational)

- To be the hero who "finally fixed fundraising"
- To get back to coaching, teaching or directing
- To never again have a freezer full of cookie dough or a garage full of popcorn
- To make sure no kid sits out over money
- To look organized and trustworthy in front of parents and the school

## The Parent: the one who has to share the link

Parents will gladly pay to support their kid, but they hate selling, and they feel embarrassed asking people for money. Your product lives or dies on whether a parent feels okay sharing their kid's page.

### Pains

- **Selling junk to friends and coworkers.** Parents describe product fundraisers as overpriced items their own families don't want, and some are embarrassed to show the catalog to grandparents ([Scary Mommy](https://www.scarymommy.com/parenting/school-unfundraiser-reddit)).
- **No time.** Two working parents have no hours for pitching, collecting cash and delivering orders. Many simply write a check instead.
- **Fundraiser pile-up.** Sports, school, band and scouts all ask at once. One parent called the constant asks completely out of hand.
- **Social awkwardness.** Asking your boss, neighbors or sister-in-law for money feels like begging. Some parents worry it teaches kids entitlement.
- **Pressure on the kid.** Quotas and leaderboards make kids feel left out or ashamed when the family can't sell much.
- **Distrust of the middleman.** Parents have been suspicious of what fundraising companies do with the contact emails their kids hand over ([Software Finder reviews](https://softwarefinder.com/nonprofit/snap-raise/reviews)).
- **Rising costs.** Dues, travel and gear keep climbing, and fundraising is the only lever many families have.

### Wants

- One link to text to family, no forms, no product, no delivery
- To know exactly where the money goes and how much reaches the team
- Their kid's photo and story on a page they're proud to share
- No pressure to hit quotas in public
- Privacy: their email and their donors' emails kept off public pages

### Desires

- To give their kid every opportunity, no matter what it costs
- To be a "good team parent" without giving up their evenings
- To see grandparents and family cheer their kid on from far away
- Relief: "I shared it once and it worked"

**The insight to build on:** when schools offered parents a simple "opt out and just donate" option, it went viral and was praised as a breath of fresh air ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/alabama-schools-hilarious-opt-out-fundraising-letter-goes-viral.amp)). Parents are begging for a direct, honest ask. That is exactly what a FundYourPal page is.

## The Donor: grandparents, aunties, coworkers, local businesses

Donors are happy to give to a kid they know, and annoyed at being made to buy things they don't want. Many say outright they'd rather just be asked for money.

### What they're thinking

- "Why not just ask me for money instead of selling me candy I don't need?" ([Cathe forum](https://www.cathe.com/forum/threads/flooded-by-all-different-kinds-of-donation-requests.226471))
- "If I buy this, the team probably gets only a few dollars of it."
- "How much of my $25 actually reaches the kid?"
- "I don't want to create an account or download anything."
- "I'll do it for my grandkid, but not for every coworker's kid." Some workers set a blanket rule: I won't buy from your kids, you don't buy from mine ([SavingAdvice](https://www.savingadvice.com/forums/forum/financial-chit-chat/general-discussion/65637-?p=551018)).
- "Was that 'tip' box already checked?" Donors are growing wary of pre-selected add-ons; a 2026 lawsuit against Snap! Raise centers on exactly that ([ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/snap-raise-siphons-money-from-online-fundraising-campaigns-class-action-lawsuit-claims)).

### Donor segments

| Segment | Motive | What they need to see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grandparents and close family | Love, pride, staying connected from afar | The kid's photo, story, schedule; an easy card checkout |
| Extended family and family friends | Obligation plus goodwill | A clear goal and a quick $25 button |
| Coworkers and acquaintances | Politeness, reciprocity | No pressure, no product, fast |
| Local businesses | Visibility and community goodwill | A sponsor tier with logo, link and a QR poster at the field |
| Alumni and community | Pride in the program | Team page, progress bar, scholarship fund |

### Desires

- To feel generous and close to the kid
- To be thanked and to see the impact (the trip happened, the uniforms arrived)
- To trust that nobody in the middle is taking a big cut
- A tax receipt when the club is a nonprofit (your FAQ covers this; make it visible)

## Competitive landscape and where you win

You beat Snap! Raise and product sales on fees, but you are not the cheapest option, so don't fight on "lowest fee." Fight on **simple, honest pricing, money straight to you, built for every kid all season.**

| Option | Fee model (as published) | Their weakness | Your angle against it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product sales (cookie dough, popcorn, discount cards) | Team keeps only a share of each sale | Parents hate selling; donors hate overpaying; inventory and deliveries | "Nothing to sell, store or deliver" |
| Snap! Raise | Up to 20% platform fee, donors also asked to cover processing ([Zeffy](https://www.zeffy.com/compare/click-pledge-vs-snap-raise)) | Big cut, reps, contact-list collecting, 2026 class action on donor add-ons | "10% flat vs up to 20%. No reps. No pre-checked tips." |
| Vertical Raise | Takes a percentage; focused on product fundraisers ([99Pledges](https://99pledges.com/99pledges-vs-vertical-raise/)) | Fee transparency complaints | "One fee, in plain words, on our homepage" |
| 99Pledges | 0% platform fee, card fees apply; groups keep about 96–98% ([99Pledges](https://99pledges.com/snap-raise-alternatives/)) | Built around one-off "a-thon" events | "A page for every kid, all season, not one event" |
| GoFundMe | No platform fee on standard donations; card fees apply ([Harness](https://goharness.com/blog-posts/snap-raise-alternatives)) | One page per campaign, not per kid; no roster, sponsors or schedule | "Built for teams: roster, kid pages, sponsors, schedule" |
| Givebutter / Zeffy | Free or tip-based | General nonprofit tools, not youth-team native | "Made for coaches, live in 5 minutes" |
| Cash and checks | Free | Theft risk, no records, treasurer burden | "Every dollar recorded, straight into your account" |

### Your strongest differentiators, in order

1. **Money goes straight into your own Stripe account.** FundYourPal never holds funds. In a year of booster-theft headlines and a lawsuit about hidden fees, this is your trust weapon.
2. **5 minutes, self-serve, no sales call.** Most rivals push reps, demos or fixed campaigns.
3. **One flat 10% total. We even pay the Stripe card fees, there's no monthly fee, and donors can cover the 10%.** Easy to explain to a board.
4. **A page for every kid, all season.** Goals, sponsors, schedule and scholarships in one place.
5. **Private by default.** Parent and donor emails never shown publicly, which answers the contact-list worry.

**Watch out:** at 10%, free and low-fee tools undercut you on price, so never claim "lowest fees." Lead with convenience and trust: kid pages, 5-minute setup, money straight to your account. Claim "10% total, and we even cover the card fees" instead.

## Direct response playbook at a 10% fee

At 10%, the fee is a line in your FAQ, not your headline. You still beat Snap! Raise's up-to-20%, but free tools beat you on price. So your copy sells the **mechanism** (money straight into the club's bank), the **outcome** (goal hit with no selling), and the **risk reversal** (pay nothing unless money comes in).

### Where your buyer's head is

Most organizers already know online fundraising exists and have heard many "keep more of what you raise" claims. A tired claim needs a new reason to believe it. Match the message to how aware the reader is:

| Reader | What they believe | Lead with | Example opener |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hates fundraising, hasn't looked for a fix | "Fundraising is just misery we put up with" | The pain, then a story | "Raise your hand if you have cookie dough from last season still in your freezer." |
| Knows online tools, hasn't picked one | "They're all the same, and they take a cut" | Your mechanism | "Most fundraising apps collect your money first. Ours never touches it." |
| Using Snap! Raise or a rival | "It works, the fee just stings" | A comparison and the switch | "Same kid pages. A smaller cut. Money straight to your bank." |
| Ready to start | "Is setup a pain?" | Speed and the call to action | "Your team can be live before Saturday's game." |

### Your unique mechanism: the Direct-to-Club model

Give the Stripe payout a name and repeat it everywhere. "Direct-to-Club" (or similar) turns a technical detail into a reason to believe. Every donation goes into the club's own account, so:

- no middleman holding funds and no waiting for a check from a vendor
- every dollar has a record, which protects the treasurer from suspicion
- donors see exactly where their money lands

This is the claim free tools and Snap! Raise aren't leading with, and it lands hard in a year of booster-theft headlines.

### How to frame the 10%

- **Never lead with "10%."** Lead with the outcome, then reveal the fee as fair and simple.
- **"You only pay when you raise."** No monthly fee, no setup cost, no contract. Zero risk to try.
- **Donor-covered fees are the big lever.** When the donor covers the fee, the club receives the full gift. Givebutter reports that over 95% of its donors choose to cover fees ([Givebutter](https://givebutter.com/blog/is-snap-raise-worth-it)); test that on your own checkout before quoting a number. Keep the box unchecked by default: a pre-checked add-on is exactly what the 2026 Snap! Raise lawsuit attacks, and "we'll never pre-check it" is a trust claim in itself.
- **Compare to the right enemy.** Against Snap! Raise, show the math (10% vs up to 20%). Against product sales, compare effort and hours, not just percentages. Never compare against free tools on price.
- **Count volunteer time.** "What's a Saturday worth?" Product sales and cash collection cost the club dozens of volunteer hours; your product costs a few minutes.

### Proof you already have

Your first program is your best ad. Bartram Bears shows $6,525 raised from 151 donations, about $43 per gift, across 177 participant pages, with most gifts from grandparents and family. With permission, turn it into a short case study: the setup time, the first weekend's donations, and one quote from the organizer and one from a grandparent.

### Offer structure for ads and landing pages

1. **Headline:** the outcome or the pain ("Fund the whole season without selling a single thing")
2. **Mechanism:** Direct-to-Club, money never touches us
3. **Proof:** live donation feed and the Bartram Bears numbers
4. **Risk reversal:** no monthly fee, no contract, pay only when you raise, donors can cover it
5. **Bonuses that remove effort:** parent text script, QR poster, sponsor ask template, "no kid sits out" scholarship setup
6. **Urgency tied to the calendar:** "Get every kid's page live before your first game / winter season / spring tryouts"
7. **Call to action:** "Start in 5 minutes"

### Headlines to test

- "The fundraiser parents don't hate."
- "Nothing to sell. Nothing to deliver. Nothing to count."
- "Your team's money goes to your team's bank. Not ours. Not ever."
- "Grandma wants to help. Give her a link, not a catalog."
- "Every kid gets a page. Every dollar gets a receipt."
- "A 10% fee, not 20%. We even pay the card fees. None of the reps."
- "Set it up at halftime. Fund the season by the weekend."

The "10%, not 20%" headline compares total cost: FundYourPal's 10% includes card processing, which FundYourPal pays. Other companies handle processing differently, so keep the comparison specific and dated.

### Future content rule

Use 10% in every piece. Describe it as "10% total, and we even cover the Stripe card processing fees," with no setup or monthly fee, and pair it with "donors can choose to cover our fee, so your team gets 100%." The covered card fees are a selling point: say it out loud.

### The card-fee hook

FundYourPal pays the Stripe card processing fees out of its 10%. Most platforms pass those fees to the team or the donor, so this is a rare, concrete reason to believe the "honest pricing" claim. Use it as a surprise beat, right after the fee:

- "10% total. That's right, we even cover the Stripe processing fees for your organization."
- "No 'plus processing.' No 'plus a tip.' We pay the card fees ourselves."
- "Your 10% is the whole bill. The card fees are on us."
- "Donors can cover our 10%, and we cover the card fees, so your team keeps 100%."

It works best with treasurers and boards, who have been burned by fees added at the end.

## Objections and how to answer them

Most objections are about trust and effort, not price. Answer them on the homepage, in the FAQ and in ad comments.

| Objection | Who raises it | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Is this legit? I've never heard of you." | Organizer, donor | Money goes into your club's own Stripe account, not ours. Show the live program page and the donation feed. |
| "Free platforms exist. Why pay 10%?" | Treasurer, board | 10% is the total: FundYourPal pays the Stripe card processing fees, so nothing is added on top. Donors can choose to cover the 10%, so the team gets 100%. You also get kid pages, roster import, sponsors and schedule built for teams. |
| "We don't have a Stripe account." | Organizer | It's required; show a short how-to and the time it takes. Stripe setup is the most likely drop-off point in onboarding. |
| "Parents won't share it." | Organizer | Give them a ready-made text and social script plus a QR poster. One share to family is enough to start. |
| "Are donations tax-deductible?" | Donor | Only if your club is a qualifying nonprofit; FundYourPal itself is not a charity. Say this plainly. |
| "Will you sell our families' emails?" | Parent | Parent and donor emails are never shown on public pages; state your no-selling policy outright. |
| "Is my kid's info safe online?" | Parent | Parents claim and control their kid's page; keep photos and details optional. |
| "We already do a popcorn sale." | Organizer | Run both this season and compare what each kept after costs and volunteer hours. |
| "What if we don't hit the goal?" | Organizer | No minimums, no contract, no monthly fee; you keep every donation that comes in. |

The tax and Stripe answers above match what your site says. The no-selling policy and "no minimums" are worth confirming against your Terms before you use them in ads.
