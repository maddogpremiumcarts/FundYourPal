# Coach Ben Interview and Voice Guide

Oct 22, 2026

Coach Ben is a fictional composite built from our audience research, not a real interview. Use his answers to set the voice; confirm the big claims with real coaches.

## Meet Coach Ben

Ben Morales, 46, has coached youth baseball for 14 years and has run every kind of fundraiser there is. He's practical, a little burned by vendors, and loyal to anything that saves him time and keeps parents off his back.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Head coach, 12U travel baseball; board member of the town rec league |
| Day job | Facilities manager for a school district; coaches nights and weekends |
| Family | Married, two kids; his youngest plays on the team |
| Program | 14 players, about $28,000 a season (tournaments, hotels, uniforms, field rental, two scholarship kids) |
| Fundraising history | Cookie dough, mulch sales, a raffle, sponsor banners, one Snap! Raise campaign, one GoFundMe for a Cooperstown trip |
| Phone habits | Team group text, Facebook, the GameChanger app, email only when he has to |
| Personality | Direct, dry sense of humor, allergic to sales pitches, fiercely protective of his players |
| Motto | "I'm a coach, not a salesman." |

## The interview

### Part 1: His world

**Q: Walk me through a season. Where does the money go?**

Ben: Tournaments eat most of it. Six or seven a summer at $800 to $1,200 each, plus a hotel block we're forced to book through the tournament's travel company. Then uniforms, field rental for winter workouts, umpires, baseballs. Baseballs alone, you wouldn't believe it. All in, about $28,000 for 14 kids. That's $2,000 a family before gas and food.

**Q: And what happens if a family can't pay $2,000?**

Ben: That's the part that keeps me up. I've got two kids this year whose families just can't. One of them is maybe the best shortstop I've coached. I'm not cutting a kid over money. So we fundraise, and I quietly move some of it to cover those two. Nobody else needs to know who.

**Q: Who does the fundraising work?**

Ben: Me and two moms. Every year. There are 14 families and I could tell you before the season which three will do anything. The rest mean well. They're busy.

### Part 2: What he's tried

**Q: Tell me about the worst fundraiser you've run.**

Ben: Cookie dough, 2019. I had 60 tubs in my garage fridge in July because three families never picked up their orders. My wife still brings it up. We made maybe $1,400 and I lost two weekends sorting boxes.

**Q: And the best?**

Ben: Sponsor banners, honestly. The pizza place, a dentist, a roofing guy whose kid played for me. $300 to $500 each, a banner on the outfield fence, done. Problem is I have to chase them every year, collect checks, and get the banners printed. It works, but it's a part-time job.

**Q: You've used Snap! Raise. What was that like?**

Ben: It raised money, I'll give them that. A rep showed up at practice in a beanie and made the kids type in 20 email addresses each. Two parents emailed me upset about it. Then I found out they kept a fifth of everything. I did the math: $19,000 raised, and a big chunk never reached us. Then grandparents complained about extra charges at checkout. My mother-in-law called me about it. That was the end.

**Q: And GoFundMe?**

Ben: We used it for Cooperstown. Fine for one big ask. But it's one page for the whole team, so nobody's grandma sees *her* grandson. And a stranger commented asking why we couldn't pay for our own vacation. That stung. It's not a vacation. These kids worked three years for it.

### Part 3: What he actually wants

**Q: If you could design the perfect fundraiser, what would it look like?**

Ben: Every kid gets a page with their picture. Parents send it to family. Money goes in our account, not some company's. I can see at a glance who's raised what, so I know which families need a nudge and which kids need help. Local businesses can sponsor right on the page. And I never touch a tub of cookie dough again.

**Q: How much time are you willing to give it?**

Ben: To set it up? Half an hour, max, on a weeknight after my kids are in bed. If it needs a phone call with a rep, I'm out. I don't have time for a demo. Show me it works and get out of my way.

**Q: What matters most: fees, ease, or how much you raise?**

Ben: How much lands in our account. That's the only number I care about. Low fees don't matter if parents won't share it. Easy doesn't matter if half disappears.

### Part 4: Reacting to FundYourPal

*The interviewer shows Ben the FundYourPal homepage.*

**Q: What jumps out first?**

Ben: "Donations land directly in your club's Stripe account." That's the line. After the Snap! Raise thing, that's what I'd want to know first. Second is "Start in 5 minutes. No approval call." Thank you.

**Q: The fee is a flat 10%, card processing included, no monthly fee, and donors can choose to cover it. Reaction?**

Ben: Fair. Half of what Snap! took. If grandparents cover the fee, we get the full gift and that's the end of the discussion. I'd want to know it's 10% *total*. I've been burned by "plus processing" before.

**Q: Above $25,000 a season, the rate drops to 7%.**

Ben: Now that I like. Usually it's the opposite: the more you raise, the more they take. Our board would notice that. Our rec league raises more than my travel team, so they'd care even more.

**Q: What makes you skeptical?**

Ben: Two things. One, I've never heard of you. One program on board? That's thin. I'd want to talk to that coach. Two, Stripe. I don't know what a Stripe account is. Is that a bank? Do I need our treasurer? Our EIN? If that part's confusing, I'll close the tab.

**Q: What would make you trust a new company?**

Ben: Another coach telling me it worked. A real team's page I can click on. And the money thing: if it goes straight to our bank, you can't run off with it. That's what makes a new company safe.

**Q: What would make you tell other coaches about it?**

Ben: If my parents didn't complain. That's the bar. If grandparents found it easy, nobody got spammed, and we hit our number, I'd tell every coach in our league. Coaches talk.

### Part 5: How he talks

**Q: How would you describe FundYourPal to another coach in one sentence?**

Ben: "Every kid gets a page, families share it, the money goes straight to your account, and you never sell a thing."

**Q: Any words that turn you off in marketing?**

Ben: "Revolutionize." "Empower." "Seamless." "Maximize your fundraising potential." Anything that sounds like a sales guy. And don't call my players "participants." They're kids. They're my players.

**Q: Last question. What should we never forget about coaches like you?**

Ben: We're doing this for free, on top of a job, for kids who aren't all ours. Respect that time. Don't waste it, don't spam our families, and don't take more than you have to.

## What stood out

Ben's one number is **how much lands in our account**. Every piece of copy should point there, not at fees or features.

| Insight | What Ben said | What it means for copy and product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The quiet scholarship | He secretly covers two kids who can't pay | "No kid sits out" is emotional fuel; make the scholarship fund private and easy |
| Three families do everything | Him and two moms, every year | Sell the relief of "parents share once, the page does the rest" |
| Direct-to-account is the trust line | It's the first thing he noticed | Put it in the headline; it also answers "I've never heard of you" |
| 10% must mean 10% total | He's been burned by "plus processing" | Always say "10% total, card fees included" |
| The volume break surprised him | "Usually it's the opposite" | Lead with it for leagues and boosters |
| Grandma needs *her* grandkid | GoFundMe had one page for the whole team | The per-kid page is a real difference; show a grandparent's view |
| Stripe is the scary step | He doesn't know what Stripe is | Explain it as "the free payment account that sends donations straight to your team's bank"; publish a 3-minute walkthrough and an EIN checklist |
| Social proof is thin | "One program? That's thin." | Feature the Bartram Bears coach on video; collect testimonials fast |
| "Participants" is a cold word | "They're my players" | Use "players," "athletes," "kids" in sports copy; rename "participant pages" on the site |
| Defend the trip | A stranger called Cooperstown a vacation | Help coaches show the kids' effort and the cost, so donors see a goal, not a holiday |
| The bar for referral is low and clear | "If my parents didn't complain" | Spam-free, simple checkout is your growth engine; build a "tell another coach" moment after goals are hit |

**Product notes from the interview:** a private scholarship fund, a plain-English Stripe guide, sponsor tiers coaches can sell in one visit, and a coach dashboard showing who has shared and raised.

## Voice guide

FundYourPal sounds like **the assistant coach who has run every fundraiser and will tell you straight what works.** Not a software company, not a charity, not a cheerleader.

### Four voice traits

| Trait | We are | We are not | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight-talking | Plain, specific, honest about fees | Salesy, hyped, vague | "10% total. Card fees included. That's it." |
| Coach-to-coach | Peer who's been in the dugout | Corporate expert talking down | "You've got enough to do. This part takes 5 minutes." |
| Protective | On the side of kids, families and grandparents | Pushy with donors | "No spam. No surprise charges. Not ever." |
| Dry, warm humor | A knowing grin about cookie dough and the same three volunteers | Jokey, cute, emoji-heavy | "Your garage fridge called. It wants its space back." |

### How the voice flexes

| Audience | Dial up | Dial down | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coaches and boosters | Directness, time savings, money in your account | Emotion | "Every player gets a page. Money goes straight to your team's bank. You're live tonight." |
| Parents | Relief, dignity, no pressure | Numbers | "Share it once with family. That's your whole job." |
| Grandparents and donors | Warmth, the kid's name, trust | Jargon | "Cheer on Maya this season. Every dollar goes to her team." |
| Boards and treasurers | Records, fees, the volume break | Humor | "Every donation recorded. Rate drops to 7% above $25,000." |

### Ben's rules (non-negotiable)

1. Respect the coach's time: get to the point in the first line.
2. Lead with where the money goes.
3. Say the fee plainly, and say "total."
4. Call them players, athletes or kids, never participants or users.
5. Never pressure a parent or a donor; no quotas, no guilt.
6. Show proof, don't claim it: a real team, a real number, a real grandparent.
7. Talk like the sideline, not the boardroom.

## Style guide

The mechanics that keep every writer, freelancer and AI tool sounding the same.

### Sentences and structure

- Lead with the point. Headline first, reason second, proof third.
- Short sentences: aim for under 15 words. Fragments are fine ("No selling. No spam.").
- One idea per paragraph; two or three sentences max.
- Write at about a 6th–8th grade reading level.
- Use "you" and "your team." Use "we" sparingly, and only for promises.

### Word choices

| Write | Not |
| --- | --- |
| players, athletes, kids, dancers, performers | participants, users, members (in sports copy) |
| your team's bank, your account | payouts, disbursements, funds settlement |
| fee, 10% total | platform fee structure, processing rate |
| set up, start, share | onboard, deploy, leverage |
| fundraiser, raise money | campaign, monetize, drive revenue |
| coach, team parent, booster club | administrator, organizer account |

**Banned words:** revolutionize, empower, seamless, game-changer, maximize your potential, synergy, cutting-edge, best-in-class, "crush your goals."

### Numbers and money

- Use numerals: 5 minutes, 14 players, 10%.
- Dollars without cents unless needed: $25, $28,000.
- Always pair the fee with what's included: "10% total, card fees included, no monthly fee."
- Show the math when comparing; never round in our favor.
- Use "up to" when comparing to competitor fees that vary.

### Capitalization and punctuation

- Sentence case for headlines and buttons: "Start your fundraiser," not "Start Your Fundraiser."
- Product name: **FundYourPal**, one word, capital F, Y and P.
- Age groups as players say them: 12U, 14U.
- Exclamation points: at most one per piece, saved for a goal being hit.
- No ALL CAPS, no emoji in headlines; one or two emoji allowed in social posts to parents.

### Calls to action

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Start in 5 minutes | Get started today! |
| See a live team page | Learn more |
| Give to \[player's name\] | Donate now |
| Share with family | Spread the word |
| Set up your team tonight | Book a demo |

### Imagery and examples

- Real fields, gyms, studios and kitchens, not stock-photo stadiums.
- Show phones in hands at the sideline; show grandparents giving from home.
- Every example uses a believable team: "Riverside 12U Blue," "Maya J.," "$5,000 goal."
